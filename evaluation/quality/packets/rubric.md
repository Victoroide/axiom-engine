# AP Computer Science A rating rubric

Score each dimension using what the learner asked, the response, and the available evidence. Examples in this table come from the separate calibration packet. A shortened or alternate calibration answer illustrates a score between worked examples.

| Dimension | 0 · Materially wrong or unusable | 1 · Partly supported or incomplete | 2 · Supported and usable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `factual_accuracy` | The response contains a material falsehood about Java or course scope. CAL-02 asserts the wrong De Morgan equivalence. | The main claim is sound but a material qualification is missing. A shortened CAL-01 answer explains truncation and leaves out the effect of widening an operand. | The claims and necessary conditions are accurate. CAL-01 contrasts `7 / 2` with `(double) 7 / 2` and explains the different results. |
| `prerequisite_rationale` | A required dependency is reversed or unjustified. CAL-03 requires writing overloaded constructors before understanding what an object is. | The prerequisites are plausible, with little explanation of how they support the target. A shortened CAL-01 answer lists Data Types and Expressions without explaining their relationship. | The order and rationale support the learner's task. CAL-01 explains how operand types prepare the learner to trace division. |
| `actionable_guidance` | The guidance is unusable, contradictory, or too vague to act on. A CAL-04 variant tells the learner only to practice Java. | The guidance points in a useful direction but omits an input, step, or check. CAL-03 suggests coding a constructor without a concrete input or check. | The learner has a concrete next step and a way to check it. CAL-05 asks the learner to trace `substring(1, 4)` on `"axiom"`. |
| `resource_relevance` | The material covers a different topic or is unusable in the requested format. CAL-04 links to a podcast on database normal forms for an ArrayList task. | The material helps with a related skill or only part of the task. The CAL-01 video shows arithmetic precedence using only integer operands. | The supplied material directly serves the target and matches the requested format. The CAL-01 diagram traces integer and floating-point division side by side. |

- Record a critical judgment on every completed response. Set `critical_error` to `true` and specify `factual`, `prerequisite`, or `both` when an error would teach a substantially wrong concept or dependency. Use `false` and `none` when you identify no critical error in the available evidence. Consider critical errors independently of the ordinal scores.
- Abstain with a reason when the available context or evidence cannot support an ordinal judgment. In CAL-06 the external helper and the linked diagram are unavailable. Use the provisional course locators to verify scope and Java claims when evidence is available.
- Score each supplied resource using its offline excerpt or description. If the evidence record gives a reason it is unavailable, abstain with a reason. When no resources are supplied, record `resource_summary_status: "na"`.
